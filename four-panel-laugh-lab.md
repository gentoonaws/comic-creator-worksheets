# A tiny-story laugh lab

Turn an ordinary moment into four panels someone else can read, smile at, and talk about. Use paper, a drawing app, or a comic tool. You do not need a GenToon account to do this exercise.

Created by GenToon, operated by Fairrium in Seoul. We want more people to make stories from their own lives and have a reason to laugh and talk together.

## One small want, one gentle surprise

Choose a fictional character and something very small they want: a quiet seat, a tidy desk, or the last cookie. Keep the joke on the situation, rather than on a real person's appearance or private life.

1. **Want:** show the character protecting, chasing, or preparing for that small thing.
2. **Obstacle:** something ordinary gets in the way.
3. **Reaction:** the character makes one visible choice. Show the action instead of explaining the joke.
4. **Surprise:** reveal a detail that changes how we read the first panel.

Each panel needs one main action. Leave room for expressions. Try reading the pictures without dialogue before adding a short line.

## An original text-plan example: The desk inspector

These are fictional planning notes, not generated images or a finished comic.

| Panel | Visible action | Optional dialogue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ari aligns a pencil, notebook, and mug with great care. | "Ready to work." |
| 2 | A tiny speck is visible on the desk. Ari leans in to inspect it. | "One more thing." |
| 3 | Ari lifts the notebook to clean underneath it. A pile of scraps spills out. | "Oh." |
| 4 | Ari neatly labels a box "Tomorrow" and slides the scraps inside. | "Ready to work tomorrow." |

The first panel promises a productive start; the last reveals a tidy way to postpone it. Change the character's habit, object, and ending to make your own scene.

## Give the reader a turn

Hand your plan to one other person before explaining it. Ask:

- What did the character want?
- What changed in the last panel?
- Which action or line was confusing?

If they do not follow the surprise, change one panel and let them read again. Laughter is not a requirement or a score. A quiet smile or a different interpretation can start a useful conversation.

For a group, swap the fourth panels of two plans and discuss how each new ending changes the story. Get permission before sharing anyone else's writing or pictures publicly.

## Keep the next comic recognizable

Use the [character continuity sheet](character-continuity-sheet.txt) to keep two or three recognizable details and record where the character and objects end up. Treat the next comic as a new scene; do not replay the same joke automatically.
