# Scene-to-Panels and Continuity Kit

An editable English planning resource for writers and teachers adapting a short scene into a comic. Use it with any comic editor, a drawing app, or paper. You can use the worksheets without an account, a backlink, or GenToon.

Created by **GenToon**, operated by **Fairrium, Seoul, South Korea**. Prepared October 9, 2026. GenToon makes comic-creation software; these resources reflect that maker perspective.

## Start with one scene

1. Open [Scene-to-panels worksheet](scene-to-panels-worksheet.txt) in a text editor. Save a copy with your own title and revision date.
2. Fill the source, cast, and scene-change fields before planning images. Use writing you own or have permission to adapt; record that permission in your working copy.
3. Map four visible moments in the grid. Four is a practice constraint: add or remove panels when your scene needs it. A reaction can deserve its own panel.
4. Read the dialogue aloud in panel order. Review what each character knows and where objects move.
5. Use [Character and episode continuity sheet](character-continuity-sheet.txt) to record the accepted ending state and what the next episode inherits.
6. Make the comic in your chosen editor, then use the export/readability checklist at the end of the scene worksheet.

[The Lantern Label: filled planning example](original-planning-example.md) shows the fields in use, including a deliberate revision. It is original fictional **text planning**, not generated-image proof, a finished comic, or a test of any editor's output. No images are included.

## For a writing group or classroom

Work offline with fictional names. One person plans the beats; another follows the panel order without hearing the source scene first. Ask them what changed and which action was unclear. Revise the plan before illustrating. Teachers can assess whether a reader can follow the actions and identify the speakers; no student software account is needed to use this kit. Keep students' personal information out of shared copies.
